Keep Your Bucks™ — How Buck billing works

Your monthly Buck charge equals your plan Bucks at 1 Buck = $1, plus a flat $5/month service fee. Any Bucks still sitting in Bucks in the Bank at month-end are automatically recalled and applied as a credit against your next invoice — so you only pay for Bucks your team actually used. 5 — STRIPE BILLING LOGIC
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Audit and correct all Stripe billing logic to match the Keep Your Bucks™ policy exactly. The following rules must be enforced end-to-end in Stripe and in the application code that drives Stripe calls:

─────────────────────────────────────────
BILLING STRUCTURE
─────────────────────────────────────────
Each org is on a monthly subscription. The invoice for a given billing cycle must be calculated as follows:

  Invoice total = (Bucks actually used by admins that cycle × $1) + $5 flat service fee

"Bucks actually used" = plan Bucks minus any Bucks recalled from Bucks in the Bank at cycle end.

The flat $5 service fee must be a fixed line item on every invoice regardless of Buck usage.

─────────────────────────────────────────
RECALL & CREDIT LOGIC
─────────────────────────────────────────
At the end of each billing cycle, before the invoice is finalized:

Step 1 — Count how many Bucks remain in Bucks in the Bank (unallocated/unused by admins).
Step 2 — Recall those Bucks back to the platform (zero out Bucks in the Bank for that cycle).
Step 3 — Calculate the credit: recalled Bucks × $1.
Step 4 — Apply that credit to the upcoming invoice using a Stripe credit note or invoice item with a negative amount. Do NOT carry it as a manual account credit — apply it directly to the next invoice line items so it is visible to the customer.
Step 5 — The invoice sent to the customer must clearly show:
  • Plan Bucks: [N] Bucks × $1 = $[N]
  • Recalled Bucks credit: -[R] Bucks × $1 = -$[R]
  • Service fee: $5.00
  • Total due: $[N - R + 5]

─────────────────────────────────────────
EMPLOYEE BUCKS — DO NOT TOUCH
─────────────────────────────────────────
Bucks that have already been awarded to employees are owned by the employee. They must never be recalled, never deducted, and must never factor into the billing calculation. Only Bucks in the Bank (unallocated pool) are subject to recall.

─────────────────────────────────────────
ORDER FULFILLMENT DEDUCTION
─────────────────────────────────────────
When the developer marks an order as fulfilled in the developer dashboard, the full Buck cost of that order is deducted from the organization's balance at that moment. This deduction must be reflected in the running Buck totals used to calculate end-of-cycle billing. Bucks are NOT deducted at the time the employee places the order — only at fulfillment.

Ensure Stripe is not charging for Bucks at order placement. Verify this logic in the webhook and invoice generation code.

─────────────────────────────────────────
STRIPE IMPLEMENTATION CHECKS
─────────────────────────────────────────
Verify and correct each of the following:

1. Subscription plan: confirm the base subscription represents the plan Buck count at $1/Buck/month plus the $5 fee as a separate metered or fixed line item.

2. Invoice finalization timing: confirm that the recall calculation (Step 1–4 above) runs BEFORE Stripe finalizes the invoice, not after. If using Stripe's invoice.upcoming webhook or a scheduled job, ensure it fires with enough lead time to apply the credit before the invoice closes.

3. Credit application method: credits must appear as a visible negative line item on the invoice (not as a Stripe customer balance/credit that silently offsets future invoices). Use a negative invoice item or a credit note applied to the specific invoice.

4. Proration: if an org upgrades or downgrades their Buck plan mid-cycle, ensure Stripe prorates correctly and the recall logic still runs on the correct plan Buck count for each portion of the cycle.

5. Failed payments: if a payment fails, Bucks in the Bank must be frozen (no new allocations allowed) until the invoice is settled. Add a check in the allocation flow that blocks new distributions if the org has an open past-due invoice in Stripe.

6. Webhook handling: confirm that all relevant Stripe webhooks (invoice.finalized, invoice.payment_succeeded, invoice.payment_failed, customer.subscription.updated) are handled and that the app's internal Buck ledger stays in sync with Stripe invoice outcomes.

7. Test this flow end-to-end in Stripe test mode using the demo account before deploying to production.
